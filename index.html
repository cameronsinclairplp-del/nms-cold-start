Your first fifty hours,
done properly.

No Man's Sky is eighteen games in a trench coat and it explains about four of them. This is the order to do things in, the settings to change before you press New Game, the two income systems that end the grind early, and the mistakes that quietly cost people their first playthrough.

Tick things off as you go — progress saves in this browser. Nothing here needs another player, a portal address, or a friend with a spare ship.

Read this first

Six rules that decide how this goes

Everything below is downstream of these. If you only take one thing, take the last one.

  • 01

    It's a marathon that looks like a sprint

    A hundred hours in still feels like the tutorial. Nothing here is missable. There is no server wipe, no season you fell behind on, no meta.

  • 02

    Fix the economy before the gear

    Almost every "this game is a grind" complaint is a nanite problem. Set up passive income in phase 2 and the rest of the game becomes shopping.

  • 03

    Stack your errands

    Never fly somewhere for one reason. A trip to a wealthy system is a freighter check, a multi-tool cabinet, an exosuit slot and a station mission — all at once.

  • 04

    The Anomaly is the answer

    Stuck on a recipe, a blueprint, a slot, a hyperdrive tier? It's on the Space Anomaly. Get there early and go back constantly.

  • 05

    Difficulty is a dial, not a decision

    Almost every setting can be changed mid-save from the pause menu. Pick Normal, start playing, adjust anything that annoys you at hour 20.

  • 06

    Don't take the free fortune

    Anything that goes in a refiner can be duplicated, and friendly strangers on the Anomaly hand beginners hundreds of millions in Starship AI Valves. Take it and you have skipped the game. Delete it, or turn multiplayer off while you're aboard.

Phase −1

Before you press New Game

Ten minutes here saves ten hours later. Four settings are locked at save creation — the rest you can move whenever you like.

Which mode

Six options appear. Only one is the right answer for a first save.

Mode What it actually is
NormalPICK THIS Balanced. Die and you walk back to your grave to recover your inventory. Everything else is a variation on this.
Relaxed Less danger, less grind, Life Support switched off entirely. Fine if survival mechanics aren't why you're here.
Survival Not a skill check — an inventory-management horror game. Tiny stacks, brutal hazard drain, ship spawns far away, death costs your inventory outright. Save it for playthrough two.
Creative Everything unlocked, no hazards, free building. Achievements don't unlock. A sandbox, not a game.
Community Expedition The live time-limited event. You don't need a separate save for it — see the Cosmos section.
Custom Every slider exposed, including ones the presets don't offer. Permadeath now lives in here as a preset rather than as its own mode.

Locked at creation, so decide now: Survival Elements, Recipes & Blueprints, Tutorial Missions, Starting Slots. Roughly twenty other settings stay adjustable from the pause menu forever.

Reroll your starting planet

You spawn on a random world with one of four hazards — cold, heat, radiation or toxicity. The universe is procedural, so restarting doesn't just reset progress, it generates a different starting world.

If you want blue skies, restart until you get a frozen planet — cold biomes always have them. It feels silly. It's also the difference between bouncing off this game and falling for it, and it costs you four minutes.

Settings worth changing in the first ten minutes

Accessibility

Hazard Effects → ~35%

Defaults to 100%, which in a blizzard at night frosts your entire screen to zero visibility. At 35% the weather still reads clearly but stays around the edges.

Controls

Hold for Important → Always Instant

Kills the hold-to-confirm delay on opening, crafting, buying and selling. Thousands of interactions become one click. Be careful for the first hour — selling is now instant too.

Optional · Difficulty

Natural Resources → Abundant

More deposits and faster gathering. If the early mining loop is what's grating on you, this is the slider — not the difficulty preset.

Optional · Difficulty

Death Consequences → keep items

Removes the corpse run. Also worth a look: Sprinting Limitation → Relaxed, if stamina-gated walking bothers you.

Optional · pure QoL

Sprint → Infinite · Scanner Recharge → Fast · Fuel Usage → Discounted

Three sliders that remove friction without touching progression. Standard scanner recharge is surprisingly slow. The one to be careful with is Recipes & Blueprints → All Unlocked — it deletes an entire layer of the game, and it's locked at save creation.

Network

PvP off, or multiplayer off entirely

PvP is rare and unbalanced. Turning multiplayer off makes the Anomaly a quiet NPC-only hub — which also removes the duplication problem in rule six. You can flip it back on any time.

Graphics · PC 2026

Clouds: Ultra → High

The cheapest win on PC — around 10–12% frames back for a difference you won't see. Ultra shadows aren't worth it over High either. 7.0 supports DLSS 4.5 with frame generation and Intel XeSS 3; HDR and frame gen now work together.

The one that catches everyone: ship roll is on the bumpers, and so is the Pulse Engine. New players constantly launch into pulse while trying to bank.

How to read the pad, structurally

  • The D-pad is the menu cluster — up is Build, down is Quick Menu, left/right cycle HUD and modes.
  • Both sticks click for the two most-used verbs: LS scans, RS sprints, zooms and dismantles.
  • X is the universal interact. If a guide says "press E", press X.
  • The Quick Menu (D-pad ↓) is the gateway to everything PC gives a dedicated key: recharging, summoning, the galaxy map, ejecting into a spacewalk.

Pad gotchas on PC

  • Plug the pad in before you launch. Controllers connected after launch have a long history of not being detected at all.
  • Broken or missing button glyphs mean a stale Steam Input layout. Reload the official Hello Games config from Remap Controls → Configure Gamepad via Steam → Recommended.
  • If the pad does nothing at all, try Steam → game Properties → Steam Input Per-Game Setting. Both "Forced On" and "Forced Off" fix it for different people — try both.
  • Ship pitch inversion lives under Flight Controls (Tethered / Locked, each with an Inverted variant), not a separate pad toggle.

Accuracy note. Hello Games' own published pad layout is watermarked 4.0, and update 6.0 "rearranged a number of input bindings". These are the best-corroborated mappings available, but check anything marked unconfirmed against your own Remap Controls screen before you rely on it.

The roadmap

Seven phases, in this order

Each phase solves one problem and unlocks the next. Do them roughly in sequence — the game will happily let you skip ahead, and that's how people end up forty hours deep with no money and no idea what they're doing.

Overall 0/0
0 Hour 0–3

Don't die, and stop mining like a caveman

Follow the tutorial. It teaches the core systems without drowning you. But do these things alongside it — they change the feel of the entire early game.

Done when

Your ship flies, you have a Terrain Manipulator, and you're holding at least 10 Salvaged Data.

0/0

  • Put the Mining Beam in a supercharged slot

    Open your multi-tool inventory before anything else and drag the Mining Beam into the highlighted slot. Mining goes from about 40 units a tick to 50. Over several hundred nodes that's real time.

  • Pulse the trigger instead of holding it

    The mining beam mines faster as it heats up. Tap-firing keeps it hot without tripping the overheat cooldown. Its range is also far longer than it looks — you rarely need to walk over.

  • Learn the six resources that matter

    Oxygen life support · Sodium hazard protection and ship shields · Carbon multi-tool fuel and building · Ferrite Dust crafting · Di-hydrogen launch fuel · Cobalt from caves. Ferrite + Cobalt crafts Ion Batteries in your inventory, which fully restore hazard protection.

  • Harvest pods individually, not the whole plant

    Walking up to a bulb plant and picking each pod gave around 173 Oxygen. Shooting the whole thing gave 43. Roughly four times the yield for ten extra seconds.

  • Melee-dash everywhere

    While sprinting, melee then jetpack — RS → RB → A. You launch forward far faster than running. Glowing deuterium plants give about ten seconds of unlimited jetpack; chain the two and you cross a continent.

  • Bind the ship to a hotkey

    Quick Menu is D-pad ↓ and it's how you recharge, summon and travel. On keyboard you can bind summon-ship to Ctrl + a number; on a pad, just get fast at the wheel.

  • Unlock the Terrain Manipulator, then hunt buried tech

    This is the moment the early game breaks open. Scan with the Analysis Visor for the wi-fi-style icons — buried Tech Modules sit near broken, smoking machinery. Face the working panel and dig to the left; it's always there. Each gives 2–4 Salvaged Data. Repairing the machinery also pays nanites.

  • Use the smallest terrain beam on finite deposits

    All three sizes give the same material per shot, but the big one eats the deposit far faster. On copper and other limited nodes, shrink it with LB — a single copper deposit can yield close to a thousand. Big beam is for tunnelling only.

  • Touch every save beacon you pass

    Nearly every point of interest has a "Save and Chart" pole. It saves the game and hands you free Navigation Data — the currency for charts, which is how you'll expand your exosuit later.

  • Know that the first storm is fake

    Your starting planet on Normal has no real weather. The first storm is scripted to teach tension — walk through it. Later storms are real, and each carries a hidden buff: blizzards reduce beam overheating, firestorms cut jetpack fuel use, radiation storms boost mining yield, toxic storms extend sprint. Press D-pad ← to cycle the HUD messages that tell you which.

1 Hour 3–8

Get off the rock and reach the Anomaly

Push the primary missions until the Space Anomaly opens up. It is the single most important location in the game and it answers nearly every "how do I make this" question you will ever have.

Done when

You can summon the Anomaly from anywhere in space, and you have a base with a powered teleporter.

0/0

  • Sell ~10 Salvaged Data at your first station — then stop

    At roughly 52,000 units each that's about half a million, which buys the tutorial materials instead of mining them. After that first sale, hoard it. Salvaged Data is the only currency the Construction Research Station accepts, and you will want hundreds of it.

  • Re-select your tracked quest after picking up any new one

    The single most confusing thing in this game: it auto-switches your tracked mission to whatever you picked up last, and untracked quests often show no markers and refuse to progress. If a mission seems broken, it's almost certainly this.

  • Scan a planet from space before you land

    Look at it and hit click LS. You get the biome type and the full resource list. This is how you find copper — and copper refines into Chromatic Metal, which an enormous number of blueprints demand.

  • Plant a small base on a copper planet

    Four walls, a roof, a Base Computer, and a Base Teleport Module powered by a solar panel and battery. That's permanent fast travel plus a storm shelter — standing inside your own base refills hazard protection. Do this on any planet holding a resource you'll want again.

  • Take the free exosuit slot at every space station

    One per station on first visit, plus one more per system at the Anomaly's expansion terminal. Early on take cargo, not tech — you can't fill tech slots yet. Aim for roughly 50/50 later. Ceiling is 180 slots: 120 cargo, 60 tech.

  • Walk the whole Anomaly once without unlocking anything

    The Nexus for missions and quicksilver, the Expedition Terminus, the research terminals, the Synthesis Lab, the Construction Research Station, the exosuit expansion terminal. Read the menus. You'll come back here hundreds of times.

  • Unlock almost everything in the Synthesis Laboratory

    These are the crafting recipes for intermediate materials you'd otherwise have to buy. Being self-sufficient out in the black is worth far more than the nanites. Grab Atlas Pass v1 immediately — the little red barrels it opens are everywhere and usually hold something useful.

  • Visit Iteration: Helios and Iteration: Ares each day

    Helios pays nanites once a day for new discoveries, and later gives one free Emergency Signal Scanner a week for derelict freighters. Ares pays 50 nanites per Journey Milestone. Scan everything you walk past — every plant, rock and animal is units now and milestone nanites later.

  • Buy the Advanced Mining Laser and Optical Drill

    The Advanced Mining Laser unlocks the deposits marked as needing it — including Radiant Shards, which you'll need for a Sentinel Interceptor. The Optical Drill sharply raises yield, and in a supercharged slot it nearly doubles your mining output.

  • Grab the Launch Auto-Thruster when you can afford it

    It recharges your launch thruster whenever the ship is parked. After this you effectively never manage launch fuel again — and you can always summon your ship, which you can't do on an empty tank. Every takeoff burns 25% of the charge, so this is a bigger deal than it sounds.

2 Hour 8–15 · the important one

Stop being poor, permanently

Nanites gate every upgrade in the game and units gate every ship. Solve both now, passively, and the next forty hours become shopping instead of grinding. This is the phase people skip and then complain the game is a grind.

Done when

You have 10–15 fish traps across a few water planets and you stop thinking about nanites.

0/0

  • Unlock Automated Fish Traps — they cost one Salvaged Data

    Construction Research Station → Tech Modules. Build cost is 50 Salt, 3 Metal Plating, 1 Ion Battery — all of which you already know how to make. No bait, no weather requirement, no biome requirement. Three per planet.

  • Build a fishing network across several water planets

    Fifteen traps generates roughly 15,000 nanites or around 3 million units a day for a few minutes of checking. The three-per-planet cap isn't a limit so much as a reason to keep exploring.

  • Farm B-class fish, not C-class

    Nanites per fish run about 15–30 for C, 50–90 for B, 100–120 for A and 150–200 for S. Each trap has four hooks holding 20 C, 6 B, 1 A or 1 S. A full C hook averages ~450 nanites and a full B hook ~420 — but B fills three times faster and you release six fish instead of twenty. Clear out C, A and S so the traps refocus on B.

  • Know the catch: traps want you nearby

    They run on roughly a 15–30 minute cycle and fill far more reliably when you're actually in the area. Leaving them overnight gets you maybe a thousand nanites from three traps. Visit a few times a day; don't expect an idle farm.

  • Want units instead? Cook the fish first

    Nanites need releasing fish one at a time. Selling is instant in bulk. Grilled C-class fish can sell for up to thirty times raw. Never grill A or S-class — cooking can lower A-class value and always lowers S-class.

  • Run the drop-pod loop for exosuit slots

    Cartographer → Exchange Specific Charts → Exosuit Upgrade Chart. Buy several. Use one, pulse to the pod, claim the slot, then touch the save pole on site to get one Navigation Data back — net cost two. Roughly a slot every ninety seconds. Pre-craft one Carbon Nanotube, ten Sodium Nitrate and one Antimatter Housing per pod.

  • Reroll the mission agent for slot rewards

    Starship and multi-tool slots come from station mission agents — look for the blue starship icon (Storage Augmentation) or the multi-tool icon. Every station has a different agent, so teleport station to station and reroll the offers.

  • Install the Trade Rocket

    90 nanites at the Anomaly, runs on Tritium, takes one exosuit slot. Load junk anywhere in the galaxy and launch — units land instantly, no station trip. Don't put nanite-bearing items in it; it doesn't trade for nanites and they're gone.

  • Dig a pit before shooting Runaway Mold

    Mold balls roll away when hit. Dig a hole with the Terrain Manipulator, let them fall in, then shoot them all in place. Refine the mold into Nanite Clusters. Living Slime from derelict freighters runs about 5:1 to nanites.

  • Add a Nutrient Processor or Exo-Skiff for storage

    The Nutrient Processor takes one exosuit slot and gives you 50 slots of food storage, deployable anywhere on land. The Exo-Skiff takes no slot at all and adds 60 fish slots plus a grill — but it only deploys on water. Both cook.

  • Read the item description before you sell anything

    Hover any item and the line tells you what it's for. "Used for building, crafting, charging, upgrading" — keep it. "Used for upgrading and refining" — refine it. "Used for trading" — sell it freely. Salvage from scrapping says outright that it's only good for selling.

3 Hour 15–25

Buy the gear that changes how it feels

Now that nanites are solved, skip the tiers. Buy S-class outright and only install lower classes if they were free. Three upgrades are worth more than everything else combined.

Done when

Movement feels fast, a ship you actually like sits at S-class, and combat is no longer a problem.

0/0

  • Three S-class Movement System upgrades. Do this first.

    About 2,000 nanites total for all three, and nothing else in the game changes the moment-to-moment feel this much. Without them traversal is slow and clunky; with them you flow across terrain. Put jetpack tech in an exosuit supercharged slot.

  • Then hazard protection, then life support

    Shield Lattice raises hazard capacity, C-class Hazard Protection Units raise resistance to a specific hazard, the Haz-Mat Gauntlet lets you harvest dangerous plants, and the Personal Refiner MK-2 refines two things while you walk. Sit hazard modules next to each other for adjacency.

  • Get a Sentinel multi-tool from a Harmonic Camp

    Destroyed Dissonance Resonators drop Echo Locators roughly one time in five; corrupted quadrupeds drop them too. An Echo Locator points to a Harmonic Camp — solve the terminal puzzle and take a free Sentinel multi-tool. Its Hijacker Laser stacks with the standard mining laser for serious mining speed.

  • Stop hunting for a natural S-class multi-tool

    Any tool upgrades to S with nanites at any station's Upgrade Station. Pick a design you like the look of and pay for the class. Rerolling trick: land on a planet in the system, save and reload on the surface, then return to the station — both wall cabinets restock from different pools.

  • One primary weapon, one secondary. No more.

    Scatter Blaster for close range, Boltcaster or Pulse Spitter for rapid fire, Neutron Cannon for heavy area damage. Paralysis Mortar is a strong secondary, especially with the Voltaic Amplifier. Every extra weapon slows your switch cycle — and the mining beam and terrain manipulator are already in that rotation.

  • Get your first Sentinel Interceptor

    Find a dissonant system, then its dissonant planet, then a purple Dissonance Resonator. Beat five waves of Corrupted Sentinels and a crashed Interceptor site appears. You need 1 Inverted Mirror and 3 Radiant Shards, then interact with the Hyaline Brain, take it to the monolith, and return. Sentinel difficulty scales with your gear, so doing this early is genuinely easier.

  • Scrap the Interceptors you don't like

    Even a C-class Interceptor scraps for around 20 million units at a Starship Outfitting Terminal. This is the fastest early money in the game. Their Crimson Core also has a 600 light-year base hyperdrive range, which is enormous this early. Each dissonant planet only spawns one design, so move systems for variety.

  • Buy an S-class Solar starship at an outlaw trade outpost

    Fit a Conflict Scanner and an Economy Scanner. Filter the galaxy map to Conflict mode, find a red outlaw system, then use the economy scanner to find a Trade Outpost — traffic there is far faster than a station. Drop a save beacon on the pad, move your own ship off the pad so all five NPC ships can land, then save and reload to trigger a fresh wave. Expect an S-class within about five reloads. Solars cost roughly 5–8 million.

  • Why Solar: the Vesper Sail

    It boosts pulse drive speed and slowly recharges your launch thrusters, so early flying is smooth before you own a single upgrade. If the sails block your first-person view, take any other S-class instead — you can upgrade any ship's class with nanites, so buy the look you want.

  • Ship loadout: Infra-Knife Accelerator + Phase Beam

    The Infra-Knife delivers the burst; the Phase Beam drains enemy shields and restores yours, which makes long fights sustainable. Also fit pulse engine upgrades, a teleport receiver for remote cargo access, economy and conflict scanners, launch thrusters and aqua-jets.

  • Only install your highest hyperdrive

    The top-tier drive grants access to every lower star colour too, so early on don't waste tech slots stacking Cadmium, Emerald and Indium. Stack them later, when you have slots to spare, purely for the adjacency range bonus.

4 Hour 25–40

A fleet, a flagship and a ship you designed

This is where the game stops being survival and starts being a space empire sim. A freighter is a mobile base, a frigate fleet is a second income stream that runs while you're asleep, and a corvette is the single biggest quality-of-life jump available to you.

Done when

Five frigate expeditions go out daily and you own a corvette you built yourself.

0/0

  • Decline the first freighter if it's below A-class

    Rescue a freighter under pirate attack and the captain offers you command. The first freighter you ever accept is free — and declining doesn't burn that. Keep declining. In wealthy systems there's roughly a 30% chance of an A-class. Freighters can't be class-upgraded later, so this choice sticks.

  • But don't shop forever

    Frigates gain experience and climb to S-class through expeditions, so the earlier you deploy them the sooner they're good. Take a decent A-class and get moving. You can trade up to an S-class freighter later and carry your whole fleet and freighter base across with you.

  • Hire around 25 frigates, five of each specialisation

    Hail freighters in space; only green-icon frigates are for hire. Prices run roughly 1.5–12.5 million. Expedition types are random, so spread across combat, exploration, industrial, trade and support. Cap is 30. Don't chase S-class — they all get there eventually.

  • Check Fuel Efficiency when you hire

    It's a real selection criterion, not flavour. A bad ratio doubles what every expedition costs you — one demo went from 100 tons of frigate fuel to 50 just by swapping one ship. Support vessels reduce fleet fuel costs.

  • Build six Fleet Command Rooms

    One room lets you run one expedition group. Five expeditions refresh daily at midnight UTC with up to five frigates each — so five rooms covers it and a sixth lets you launch just before reset and briefly exceed the cap. Building the first room unlocks the frigate fuel recipes.

  • Aim two stars above the expedition difficulty

    Match frigate specialisation to the expedition type and keep your fleet rating at least two stars over the difficulty to avoid damage. Each run takes up to three consumable upgrades. Timers are real-life hours — launch before bed. A single good expedition can return close to two million units.

  • Build the Poor Man's Corvette for about 10 million

    At the Corvette Workshop Terminal buy the required basics: landing gear, habitation module, cockpit, reactor, thrusters, weapon system and accessway. Two landing gears, a Thunderbird Hab on top, Thunderbird Cockpit at the front, Titan Landing Bay at the rear, two Medusa reactors, a Pyrodrive Booster, mirrored Titan Sublight Thrusters and Photon Cannon Arrays. Set your colour scheme first — the game reuses it for every part you add after.

  • The reason to rush a corvette: scanning from the air

    A corvette holds autopilot while you leave the cockpit and use the Analysis Visor mid-flight — and the visor works straight through the hull. Mineral, gas and power hotspots, salvageable scrap and ancient bones all get spotted from above, shot from above, and looted without landing. It can beam you down and back up. It removes the land-scan-take-off-reposition loop that eats hours.

  • Fit a Mission Radar, and go easy on the interior

    A Mission Radar gives you daily quicksilver missions without flying to the Anomaly. A Refiner Unit is a free three-input refiner. Put both in the rear — cramming functional modules near the pilot seat causes interaction prompts to fight each other.

  • Link your storage containers

    Containers are numbered 0–9, and container 3 in your base, your freighter and your corvette are the same container. With cargo racks on the corvette, station vendors can pull materials straight out of them — no flying home to apply an augmentation.

  • Farm advanced corvette modules from salvageable scrap

    Scan planets from space for "Salvageable Scrap", then look for the salvage container icon in the visor and dig or shoot it out — usually 2–4 parts each. At the workshop, Purchase Modules → Advanced Modules → Make Offer, adding parts until the success percentage is near 100.

  • Optional: the corvette-for-hauler flip

    Corvettes can't be scrapped, but they can be traded to an NPC for a regular starship — which can. Build a minimal corvette, find a wealthy Gek system (they favour haulers, the most expensive regular ships), analyse landing traffic for an A-class, swap, and scrap it for 20 million-plus. Then build another.

5 Hour 30–60 · mid-game

You own a freighter and a corvette. Now make them matter.

This is the point where most players stall: big ships, baseline gear, and a freighter whose entire tech tree is locked behind a currency nobody explained. Three clocks are also running in real time — the settlement, the Living Ship, and your frigates — so start them before you do anything else.

Done when

Matter Beam installed, Indium Drive fitted, a Void Egg incubating, and your settlement in the black.

0/0

  • Start the three real-time clocks today

    Buy a Void Egg (3,200 quicksilver) and carry it while pulsing to start the Living Ship quest — its four organs each take 18–26 real hours to mature. Claim and start upgrading your settlement. Send frigates out. None of these care whether you're logged in.

  • Buy the Emeril and Indium drives now — it's 320 nanites total

    Iteration Hyperion on the Anomaly: Emeril 120 nanites (250 Cadmium + 4 Wiring Loom), Indium 200 nanites (250 Emeril + 5 Wiring Loom). Mine Cadmium in the red systems you can already reach, build Emeril, mine Emeril in green, build Indium. Then dismantle the lower drives — the highest one covers everything below it.

  • Learn the freighter's real currency: Salvaged Frigate Modules

    Nearly every freighter blueprint at the Upgrade Control costs SFMs, not nanites — roughly 90 to unlock everything. Ranked sources: station and Nexus missions that show an SFM as the reward (guaranteed), derelict freighters (many crates, ~2.5% each), industrial frigate expeditions (7–20% per run), guild envoys at high standing, and cargo-pod raiding in pirate systems where sentinels don't care.

  • Spend your first five SFMs on the Matter Beam

    It lets you push and pull freighter storage from anywhere in the system, and reach the main freighter inventory from anywhere in the galaxy. It changes how every other activity feels. Fleet Command Rooms, the Teleport Chamber, Galactic Trade Room and storage rooms are free to build — do those too. Then hyperdrive tiers as modules arrive: Amplified Warp Shielding 4 for red, Chromatic 8 for green, Temporal 12 for blue.

  • Run a derelict freighter every week

    Iteration: Helios hands you a free Emergency Signal Scanner weekly (the Scrap Dealer sells them from 5M, doubling each day). A run pays roughly 2–6M units, 1,500–4,000 nanites, SFM crates, and at the end a freighter hyperdrive unit — the only reliable source of them. Stand by the heaters, don't move while a nest is stirring, read every log.

  • Get the settlement out of debt: two Farms to S, one Warehouse to A

    Every building starts C-class and upkeep beats productivity until you fix that. Overseer's Office first, then Farms, then Warehouse, then Saloon and Marketplace. One construction at a time, stages of 20 minutes to 2 hours real time, products claimable every 20 hours. Never claim a second settlement while a build is in progress — it breaks the Overseer's Office.

  • Ghosts in the Machine: bring a Boltcaster and a stack of Chromatic Metal

    The Scientist chain needs a Manufacturing Facility door blown open, and only a Boltcaster does it. Material requests scale fast, so bank Pure Ferrite, Condensed Carbon and Chromatic Metal before you start. Build the terminals in your real base, not a throwaway one.

  • Run the Atlas Path alongside — and scan everything, because Atlas Seeds are gated by milestones

    Ask Polo where the next interface is. The ten Atlas Seed blueprints unlock at 5, 10 … 50 total Journey Milestones, so every discovery, word and warp you bank now shortens this later. Finish the Atlas Path before the last Artemis missions: it reveals black holes on the map, which makes The Purge's glyph hunt shorter. Talk to Nada each time The Space Anomaly mission updates — 150 quicksilver a pop.

  • Before The Purge: swap to a spare ship and multi-tool, and store your exosuit tech

    The galaxy-reset ending damages the technology installed in your active starship, multi-tool and exosuit. Fly the trip in a ship you don't care about, equip a spare tool, and uninstall your exosuit modules into inventory first. If you refuse the reset instead, that choice is permanent for the save.

  • Take the cheap quality-of-life unlocks you've been walking past

    Orbital Exocraft Materialiser — 1 Salvaged Data at the Anomaly, summons any exocraft anywhere in your freighter's system. Minotaur AI Pilot — 480 nanites, it follows you and fights on its own. Exocraft Summoning Station, 12 Salvaged Data, for planets where the freighter isn't. Atlas Pass v2 and v3, 250 nanites each — v3 opens everything.

  • Fight a Pirate Dreadnought — it's a free capital ship, or 5–10 million

    Kill its four engines then six anti-freighter lasers and it surrenders. Claim it as your freighter (capital class, keeps your fleet), extort it for 5–10M, or destroy it for a guaranteed S-class freighter hyperdrive unit. Hire its frigates before you board. Around one in five pirate systems always spawns the battle.

  • Upgrade the corvette's class only after you've checked its supercharged slots

    C→B is 10,000 nanites, B→A 25,000, A→S 50,000. Supercharged positions are rolled when a corvette is built, and only revealed as the class rises — upgrade once, look at the layout, and reload a restore point if it's bad. Feed it its own modules: many corvette hull parts boost stats and stack adjacency with regular ship tech.

  • Donate three items a day to the guild envoys

    Every station's envoy takes up to three qualifying items daily for +1 standing each. Merchants at Exalted (100) and Explorers at Master (40) hand out Salvaged Frigate Modules and bulkheads on a cooldown. It's a thirty-second habit that pays for the freighter tree.

6 Hour 60+

Finish the story. It's the real gate.

With income running in the background, go and play the Artemis Path properly. Not because it's mandatory — because completing it is what unlocks the best part of the game.

Done when

You have an Atlantid Drive and you're standing on a gas giant in a purple system.

0/0

  • Run the Artemis Path to the end

    Awakenings → Alone Amidst the Stars → Ghosts in the Machine → A Leap in the Dark → The First Traveller → Patterns in Time → 16/16 → The Purge → New Beginnings. The Atlas Path runs alongside it and is separate.

  • Then They Who Returned — the Autophage chain

    Finishing The Purge unlocks it. Dissonance Resonator → Echo Locator → Harmonic Camp → glyph puzzle. It also hands you the Aeron Turbojet jetpack, and opens Autophage settlements and character customisation.

  • Finish They Who Returned + the Atlas Path → In Stellar Multitudes

    Collect three Echo Seeds from Sentience Echoes on dissonant worlds, compile Memories Of Atlantid, then sideload them at an Atlas Interface. You choose the new system's primary lifeform and what its worlds are — a gas giant, a waterworld, a paradise, a nest of horrors. The Atlas then restores a purple star system from deletion.

  • Build the Atlantid Drive

    Blueprint from the Assembled Construct on the Anomaly; costs 250 Indium, 200 Atlantideum and 1 Living Glass. It's the top hyperdrive tier — it replaces Cadmium, Emerald and Indium entirely and it's the only way into purple systems.

  • Go and look at a purple system

    Gas giants, total-ocean worlds, abyssal depths miles deep. These use world-building tech carried over from Hello Games' next title. Grab a Nautilon submarine before you go. Purple systems were added to the galaxy — your existing bases and worlds weren't touched.

  • Pick up the Minotaur and its AI module

    Most exocraft are comfort and style. The Minotaur is not — piloted, it gives full protection from every hazard, and with the AI module unlocked it fights on its own and demolishes sentinels. It's also how you harvest Storm Crystals in an extreme storm without dying.

  • Take over a settlement or four

    Cartographer → Settlement Chart. Every settlement starts deep in debt; you're balancing productivity per day against maintenance per day. Once it's in the black the surplus crafts items for you — point the production lines at frigate fuel and it pays for your fleet. You can run up to four.

  • Scale into Stasis Devices and Fusion Ignitors

    The endgame economy. Each is worth over 15 million units, and a fully automated farm built on mineral, gas and power hotspots can generate billions a day. This is what all that corvette hotspot-scanning was setting up.

Tech & slots

The upgrade rules nobody explains

Four mechanics govern every piece of gear you'll ever own. Get these wrong and your ship performs worse than it did unmodified.

Supercharged slots

Highlighted slots that give whatever sits in them a large boost — commonly quoted at 25%, but it's variable by technology and often lands nearer 50%. Put your Optical Drill in one and mining output nearly doubles.

C

1

B

2

A

3

S

4

  • Counts above are for starships, multi-tools and freighters. The exosuit and exocraft always have exactly three, in fixed positions.
  • Positions are random per item but fixed for that item forever. They sit in the top tech rows and are often hidden behind sealed slots you haven't bought yet.
  • Upgrading class reveals more of them — another reason not to hunt for a natural S-class.

Adjacency bonuses

Upgrades of the same type reinforce each other when placed touching. A coloured border appears around modules that are actively boosting each other — if adjacent modules don't share a border colour, they aren't helping and can be moved.

  • Group by type: all launch thrusters together, all pulse engines together, all shields together.
  • Simple version: put the module you most want boosted in the middle so it's touched on all four sides.
  • It stacks with supercharged slots, and stacks harder when the supercharged slots are themselves adjacent.
  • Watch the stat bars while you rearrange. That's the only honest feedback the game gives you.

The overload rule

Install a fourth upgrade module of the same type and all of them stop working. Not diminishing returns — every module in the group switches off until you remove one.

  • Mixing classes for the same tech also overloads. Keep only your highest tier installed.
  • The cap counts per inventory tab, so gear with two tech tabs can hold three each.
  • Applies to upgrade modules, not to crafted blueprint technology.
  • Any guide telling you to stack six hyperdrive modules is from before update 4.0. It was six; it's three now.

X-class modules

Bought at outlaw stations or pulled from Suspicious Packets. Their stat rolls range from worse than C to better than S — a genuine gamble, and a late-game one. But there is one category worth chasing early:

Black-market Hazard Protection Modules are the only modules in the game that raise resistance to all four hazards at once, each rolling 1–10%. No S-class equivalent exists. Three good ones, stacked next to each other alongside C-class hazard units, can push you toward 80% resistance across the board — enough to stand in a severe storm and work.

Avoid

Traps that cost people their playthrough

Some of these lose you materials. One of them loses you the game itself.

The big one

Accepting duplicated gifts

Anything that goes in a refiner can be duplicated, and it's been possible for years. Friendly players on the Anomaly hand beginners Starship AI Valves worth hundreds of millions. Take it and you have skipped every system this guide just described. Delete it, or disable multiplayer while aboard.

Silent failure

The quest auto-switch

Picking up any new mission silently retargets your tracked quest. Untracked quests frequently show no markers and refuse to advance. If something looks bugged, open the log and re-select it before you go looking for a fix.

Permanent loss

Archiving a loaded ship

Moving a starship into freighter storage destroys everything in its cargo. Installed technology survives; loose materials do not. Empty it first, every time.

Counterintuitive UI

Scrapping the wrong multi-tool

The decommissioning terminal scraps the tool you currently have equipped. Swap to the one you want gone first. Same for exchanges: strip your upgrades off a multi-tool before trading it in.

Waste

Selling all your Salvaged Data

At 52,000 units apiece it's tempting. It's also the only currency the Construction Research Station takes — every base part, every industrial module, the fish traps themselves. Sell about ten to get moving, then bank the rest.

Waste

Topping up a half-full launch tank

Overfill is simply discarded. Wait until it's nearly empty. And every takeoff costs 25% of the charge, so don't land three times when one landing would do — or fit the Launch Auto-Thruster and forget the whole system.

Waste

Big terrain beam on finite deposits

Every beam size gives the same material per shot but the large one consumes the node far faster. Shrink it for copper and other limited deposits. Large is for tunnelling and for farming Silicate Powder off hillsides.

Known bug

The corvette refiner

Materials left in a corvette's onboard refiner have been reported as deleted — input and output both — when you warp or teleport to a new system. It may well be fixed by the time you read this; until you've tested it yourself, don't leave large stacks in there before travelling.

Structural

Flattening terrain under a base

Terrain regenerates. Flatten mode is fine for a quick job, but don't rely on it under anything permanent — place a floor instead, which clears terrain properly.

Careless

Nanite items in the Trade Rocket

The rocket sells for units only. Anything that would have refined into nanites is gone for good. Check the load before you launch.

Social

Rude words to aliens

Practising languages with NPCs is free nanites, units and occasionally exosuit modules. But picking a word that offends that species — anything about killing or weapons — can cost you a faction rank you spent hours earning.

Story

Taking the galaxy reset in your good ship

The Purge's reset damages the tech installed in whatever starship, multi-tool and exosuit loadout you're carrying at the time. Swap to spares and store your suit modules first. Refusing the reset is permanent for that save.

Stale advice

Activated Indium farms

Half the money guides still online say to mine it for millions an hour. It was nerfed to 165 units apiece years ago. Any guide leading with Activated Indium is describing a game that no longer exists — check its date before you follow anything else in it.

The slow one

Living off other people's coordinates

Portal addresses let you grab any ship, tool or world instantly. It works, and it hollows the game out — effort justification is real, and things handed to you feel worthless even when they're rare. Every ship you found yourself has a story attached. That's the entire product.

Update 7.0 · released 09/09/2026

You are starting the day space got rebuilt

Cosmos is the tenth-anniversary update and the largest change to space since launch — Sean Murray's own framing was that they'd never really updated space before. Every guide written before September is describing a different game between the planets.

Important for a new save

Deep-space points of interest only start appearing on your system map after your save first reaches the Space Anomaly. So phase 1 comes before any of this.

Star System Map

A proper local map from the Quick Menu showing every celestial body, station, deep-space location and unidentified signal in the system. A short tutorial mission called Signals introduces it. You can now fly to the star itself.

Shipbreaking

Colossal derelict hulks drift in deep space, and you strip them for technology and cargo before the structure fails. Push the hull integrity too far and it melts down and explodes. Corvettes get a Tractor Beam attachment that hoovers up and refines physical salvage.

Deep-Space Outposts

Alien quartermasters trading maps and salvaged material, plus a salvage-and-delivery contract terminal — a genuinely new income route. Some are Infested Outposts, overrun with biological growth that yields pathogen sacs.

Space Station Directorship

Prove yourself to a system's authorities and become the station's director — restyle the lobby, add rooms, decorate the exterior with enormous modules. A station you own, rather than a station you shop at.

Galactic Alliances

Directors can found alliances with their own name and banner. You can join up to three, teleport to alliance systems, and appear on activity leaderboards at the station core.

Orbital Construction & 6DoF

Drop a base computer anywhere in space and build on an asteroid platform or in claimed empty volume. Spacewalking now works from any ship via an eject option in the Quick Menu, with true six degrees of freedom including roll — and cold protection ticking down while you're out there.

Expedition 23 · Our Journey Continues

A ten-year retrospective, running for about six weeks

Every milestone represents a past update — launch through Cosmos — with developer commentary read by Sean Murray, kept permanently in your Catalogue afterwards. Rewards include a golden Rasamama S36, a Starbound v0.27 multi-tool built from the original concept art, a vintage Sentinel Interceptor rebuilt from pre-release trailer footage, and a diplodocus companion.

Hello Games said it begins "shortly" after Cosmos launched on 09/09. Check the Expedition Terminus on the Anomaly — if it's live, it is a genuinely good first-week activity.

How expeditions work, because it isn't obvious

  • You don't need a separate save. Either start a fresh expedition save from the title screen, or enter from your existing save through the Expedition Terminus on the Anomaly, carrying in up to 8 technologies and 24 items.
  • Rewards carry over. Pay nanites at milestones to copy your expedition ship and multi-tool to your main save. At the end, 8 technologies and 24 items transfer, and all units, nanites and quicksilver come with you. Everything else converts to currency.
  • If you started a fresh expedition save, you can convert it into a normal universe save at the end and keep everything.
  • Missing one isn't permanent. Past expeditions get re-run — the 2025 holiday event replayed four of them back to back on two-week rotations.

Four days in · verdict

Directorship is cosmetic and social — decoration, extra rooms, an alliance banner. Requirements reported so far: about 30 standing with the local race, 15 with the local guild, five completed salvage contracts, plus a units donation the guides can't agree on. Read the station core's panel; don't chase it for income.

Salvage contracts

The first is free; each extra one costs 450 Contaminated Metal. Salvage only counts once it's physically fed into the outpost compactor, and one hulk can fill several contracts. The corvette Tractor Beam (~300–350k units, needs the Gravitino Coil) makes this bearable — do not uninstall it; players report it deletes.

Patches

7.01 shipped 10/09 — vent-cargo fix, salvage physics, pulse-drive disengage near POIs, derelict exit bugs, a trade terminal at outposts. More is on the experimental branch. Community mood on the salvage loop is split between "fill five contracts a hulk" and "I didn't sign up to be a garbage man". If something here doesn't match your game, your game is right.

Things people ask in week one

Everyone on the Anomaly has better gear than me. Am I behind?
No. Almost everything you're looking at is a cosmetic reward from a past expedition or something bought with quicksilver, and you'll have access to comparable things within your first few weeks. There are no microtransactions and nothing is pay-to-win. If the fashion show bothers you, turn multiplayer off while you're aboard and the station fills with NPCs instead.
Should I chase an S-class ship or multi-tool?
Chase the design, not the class. Any starship, corvette or multi-tool upgrades to S-class with nanites at a station terminal, and upgrading also reveals more supercharged slots. Freighters are the exception — those can't be class-upgraded, which is why you decline free ones until an A-class turns up.
Save-scumming for ships feels like cheating.
Fair — it's a matter of taste, and it isn't the same thing as a duplication exploit. Reloading a restore point rerolls the NPC ship spawn table; that's just how the game generates traffic. If it sits badly with you, park at a trade outpost and wait for ships to land naturally. It takes considerably longer and you'll get there.
Do I have to follow the main story?
Not moment to moment — deviate freely. But it isn't optional in the long run: the Artemis Path is what unlocks the Autophage, Atlantid multi-tools, the Atlantid Drive and purple systems. Treat it as the gate to the best content rather than a chore, and do it once your income is running so you can enjoy it.
Why does my base power keep running out?
Every timer in this game runs on real-world clock time, including while you're not playing. A bio-fuel reactor stuffed with carbon might show 49 hours of power — that's two days of your life, not two days of play. Replace it with a solar panel and a battery and it never runs dry. Same applies to frigate expeditions and settlement production.
What's the fastest way to get somewhere on a planet?
On foot, melee-dash with the jetpack. In a ship, climb — the higher in the atmosphere you fly the faster you move, so go up, cross, come down. Between planets, the Pulse Engine turns a 98-minute cruise into about 27 seconds and routes around obstacles for you. Between systems, build teleporters at every base you care about.
Sentinels are chasing me. What do I do?
Dig straight down with the Terrain Manipulator and wait. It's an unglamorous panic button and it works every time. Later, an Atlantid multi-tool's Runic Lens grants brief invisibility, and a fully-upgraded Minotaur simply deletes them.
Is my progress safe if I change difficulty later?
Yes. Changing settings mid-save moves you into Custom mode but doesn't touch your progress. Four things are fixed at creation — Survival Elements, Recipes & Blueprints, Tutorial Missions and Starting Slots — everything else is a pause-menu slider. Achievements do stop unlocking in Creative.

The middle is the game

There's no final destination here — no raid tier, no level cap, no ending you're racing toward. A beginning where you learn the basics, a long middle of exploration and progression, and an end where you finally land the ship or the world you were hunting for. Almost all of the joy lives in that middle.

Everything above is aimed at removing the unnecessary grind — the bit where you mine ferrite by hand for three hours because nobody told you about salvaged data. It is not aimed at skipping the journey. Take the time savings; keep the discovery.
